## What the intervention actually did

In all four scored hybrid D02 runs, the intervention fired once for J1 after its first reply: package 1 retained the supplier-quoted 35-day FCA schedule instead of switching back to 28 days. Price remained €13.59, payment 14 days, and computed buyer cost remained €170,075.04. This was selected from the actual quote rather than hard-coded. It fired zero times in D01.

Observed D02 results vary from €14.00 FCA (0.681942) to €13.70 FCA (0.745128). A cost-neutral buyer concession does not guarantee a supplier price concession. The experiment tests package selection plus its conditional explanation together; it does not isolate wording from package effects.

Next useful experiment: isolate the schedule change from its wording, or improve the ranking of fresh, relevant evidence. Freeze one change at a time and repeat against A6 when shared session slots are available. Do not tune against hidden best-cost values or promote a best-of-run score.
